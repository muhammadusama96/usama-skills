# AI Creators Lessons

Lessons from Nate Herk (https://www.youtube.com/@nateherk), Sirio Berati (https://www.youtube.com/@sirioberati), and Jeff Su (https://www.youtube.com/@JeffSu). Concrete rules only — no theory. Deepened 2026-11-13 from the full transcripts of the three key videos (transcripts: ~/workspace/research_notes/transcripts/) and from rea-video breakdowns of the videos themselves.

## 1. The loop that makes everything work (Nate Herk)

- Every correction becomes a permanent rule in a skill. "Fix it, then add this to the skill so it never happens again." Never give the same note twice.
- When briefing an agent, tell it: "Ask me clarifying questions one at a time until you are 95% confident you can complete this."
- Always review before anything publishes. Automation proposes; a human (or jury) disposes.
- Before a big job, use the **Intent Check** (Jeff Su, §7): make the AI repeat your intent and propose a plan first. Correcting early is far cheaper than fixing halfway.
- **Roast Room:** keep a standing panel of AI reviewers fed with your real data (past videos, gate reports, comments) that debates a decision before you act.
- **A skill is a recipe.** Name of the dish, ingredients, steps, finished output — so the agent makes it the same way every time. Nate's example skill prompt (adapt the names): "Create me a new skill called repurpose YouTube video. It's going to create an AI social media manager that makes social media posts for LinkedIn, Instagram, and X. The user will input a YouTube video URL and wants it turned into a LinkedIn post, Instagram post, X post, and each one should have a visual that's optimized for that platform. Ask me clarifying questions one at a time until you are 95% confident that you can complete the task successfully."
- **Project structure that makes skills work:** `brand_assets/` (profile picture, logo, colours — the agent uses these in visuals), `drafts/` (one folder per platform: post text + visuals, for review), `scripts/` (the automation scripts, not loose in the root), `.env` (API keys only), and a short project instruction file (`CLAUDE.md` / `AGENTS.md`, under 150 lines — created by reading the project structure, Nate's `/init`). Keep the workspace tidy or the agent loses track of where things are.
- **Test loop:** run once, watch the output, give plain-word feedback ("add our profile picture and a blue checkmark to the carousel"), the agent fixes it AND writes the fix into the skill (a "known issues and findings" section) so it never happens again. Run it 10 times with feedback and it becomes battle-tested before it ever runs automatically.
- Nate's stack (for reference, NOT ours): Claude Code + Botato (MCP: finds inspiration from transcripts/websites/PDFs, creates visuals, schedules and posts to 9 platforms). We do not have a Botato account and do not sign up for one — our equivalents are Muse skills, the viral-reels job, and browser/connector posting.

## 2. Short-form reel format — the numbers (Nate Herk, measured on his 27 top reels)

- Speech starts within 0.1 s. No intro/outro card. File ends right after the last word.
- No dead air: pauses between phrases under ~0.2 s; natural beats after a comma 0.2–0.4 s stay. 4.0–4.7 words/second.
- **Shots 1.1–1.4 s, none over ~2.8 s. First cut at 0.6–1.0 s, on the first charged word.** Each item in a spoken list gets its own shot.
- Layouts (1080x1920, hard cuts): split B-roll top / speaker below 30–42%; full B-roll 50–55%; punch-in 5–15%. Speaker on screen ~45% of the time, never gone more than ~3.5 s. First frame is a split with the tool/brand on top.
- Story: hook in the first 3 s (claim/command naming the thing) → one line of credibility → list items shown early, blurred, as an open loop → each item: section card + one-line definition + 2–3 literal visuals + punch-in on the key line → resolution calling back to the hook visual → CTA: show the resource first, then a punch-in with the comment keyword.
- **Every shot must move. Video beats images, every time.** An AI still used as B-roll is a build error — stills only become first frames for AI video.
- B-roll preference order: (1) real footage of the thing named; (2) real screen recording, zoomed in, cursor visible; (3) animated UI mock (typing, counters); (4) AI video — concrete metaphors only ("burn your money" = a bill burning), generic "person at a computer" reads as stock; (5) highlight crops under 1 s. Use each B-roll scene once per reel. Never invent numbers or results to decorate a claim.

## 3. Captions, sound, and checking the edit (Nate Herk)

- Captions: 1–3 words per chunk, split on phrase boundaries, hard-swap, appear 2 frames before the first word. Bold all-caps, ~62–64 px. Colour on 25–45% of chunks, whole chunk only: red = wrong way/loss, teal = concept/keyword, green = action/payoff.
- **One look for the whole reel:** every caption in the same dark box with a thin light edge. Never white on light — measure the pixels behind every caption. Never over the mouth or chin, least of all in the first second.
- Music by mood (darker confident beat for claims/news, lo-fi for methods). Bed ~11 dB under the voice, ducked under speech. **SFX follow visible actions, not every cut:** soft air whoosh on a real spatial move, damped tap when a logo lands, dry click for small UI, paper for documents. No laser zaps, pitch-drop plops, sci-fi chirps, no whoosh on every cut. Master near −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP, measured on the final encoded file.
- Edit order: transcribe (word-level timestamps) → cut retakes → cut between phrases, never inside one; never speed the voice up → plan the story first (hook, proof, items, payoff, CTA) → source B-roll, one visual per noun phrase → build the composition, every shot anchored to the spoken word → mix → render draft → check → final.
- Cut rules that cost him takes: cut between phrases, never inside one — a phrase is what you say in one breath. Drop a filler only when there is silence on both sides of it; cutting "what it does is" out of "First, what it does is it transcribes" clips the word "First". A filler that runs straight into the next word stays.
- Check before delivery: re-transcribe the final render and compare words at every cut; frame strips across every cut (10 frames each side); contrast behind every caption; read the first 2.5 s frame by frame — nothing covers the mouth. Your own review is the final gate.

## 4. Consistent AI characters — the full 5-image sheet method (Sirio Berati)

Source: "She Isn't Real (The DEFINITIVE AI Influencer Pipeline)", transcript in ~/workspace/research_notes/transcripts/mCavxK7UJ84.txt. Tools named in the video: Nano Banana 2 (image editor in Enhancor), Kora Reality (fine-tuned Z Image), Seedance 2 (video: UGC mode and multi-reference mode), Enhancor V4 (skin fix + upscale), lastframe.ai (first/last frame extractor). We do NOT have accounts for these; use the method with the tools we do have (image generation + our video pipeline), and note where a step needs a tool we lack.

- **Step 0 — base image.** Real person: use a photo of them. Fictional character: generate a hyperrealistic base image first (Sirio uses Kora Reality, 9:16, HD) — the source image matters more than the prompt.
- **Step 1 — rotation sheet.** In Nano Banana 2 (2K, 16:9), upload the base image as the identity reference and prompt it to keep the face, proportions, body, hair shape and overall vibe identical, and to reformat it as a professional design board on a white background showing the character from multiple angles. Download it, duplicate it, crop it to front / side / back views.
- **Step 2 — close-up.** Same reference, prompt: "Give me a close-up of this man focusing on his facial features" (headshot).
- **Step 3 — full body.** Same reference, prompt: "Give me a full body shot and make sure that the face is clear."
- **Step 4 — side profile.** Same reference, prompt: "Give me a close-up shot of his side profile, left and right."
- **Step 5 — features grid.** Use the sheet as the source reference, prompt: "Give me a grid of close-up shots of the man's features such as the eyes, the lips, the nose, the lashes, the brows, and the hair."
- You now have 5 images: the rotation sheet, the headshot, the full body, the side profiles, the features grid. This is the character dataset — use it for everything. **For our work:** build a 5-image sheet for every locked character (Buster, Pip, the Green Valley girl, the new guard dog) and use it as the image reference for every clip — this is the upgrade from our locked text description.
- **UGC ad (one image):** Seedance 2 UGC mode takes ONE character image + ONE product image + a prompt that includes exactly what the influencer should say. Good for product-style ads.
- **Cinematic commercial (five images):** multi-reference mode takes all 5 character images + the product image + the prompt. This is where the sheet pays off — the model understands the character three-dimensionally.
- **Voice:** upload a 6-second high-quality audio clip of the voice. Paste the audio-reference line at the BEGINNING and the END of the prompt, and put lip-sync lines in the middle stating exactly what the character will be speaking. The generation clones the voice and drives lip sync from your words.
- **Extend a video:** take the last frame (lastframe.ai extracts it free), use it as the single reference in multi-reference mode, and add lines telling the model to reference the exact image and background as the source — otherwise multi-reference hallucinates new environments. Do NOT use image-to-video for this: it cannot take an audio reference, so the voice would break.
- **Place the character in a scene:** add a background image as a reference and mention it in the prompt (@image number) — e.g. a podcast-studio background for a talking video.
- Fix the "AI look": deliberately stylised beats half-real every time. Never let a character drift toward plastic realism by accident.
- Reverse-engineer hit formats into reproducible pipelines (strip a clip to its motion, keep the structure, rebuild with your own material) — and only ever with original or licensed media and documented likeness/voice/music rights.

## 5. Motion graphics and explainer boards (Sirio Berati)

Repo (MIT, cloned 2026-11-13): ~/workspace/youtube-ai-motion-graphics (github.com/sirioberati/YouTube-AI-Motion-Graphics). 74 animated boards, each one standalone HTML file, 1920×1080, CSS animations, loops every 8–12 s. Full system in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`; board list with "say it when" tags in `skills/explainer-board-system/references/board-catalog.md`.

- **How a board works:** plain HTML page, fixed pixel layout on a 1920×1080 canvas; fonts (Inter, JetBrains Mono, Instrument Serif) from Google Fonts; colours/borders/shadows from tokens; motion is CSS keyframes. The bottom-right 480×336 box stays empty for the camera. Every readable word ≥28 px. One idea per board. Nothing still for more than 5 s.
- **The pipeline (5 steps):** (1) Whisper — a timestamp for every word of the voiceover; (2) board-brief — which spoken lines need a board and what goes on it; (3) board-system — pick a board from the catalog (or build one) using the tokens and `checks.md`; (4) board-sync — every element lands on the word that names it (never before it, never more than 2 s after); (5) export — Chrome renders every frame on a virtual clock (never drops a frame), FFmpeg joins the frames with the voice into MP4. Commands: `python tools/export.py boards/D44Line.html --seconds 8 -o out/line.mp4` (one board) or `python tools/export.py timeline.json --audio my-voice.wav -o out/video.mp4 --first 60` (render the first 60 s first, check, then the full video).
- **Rules enforced in the system:** land on the spoken word; named things appear when named; structure first (boxes/rails/outlines are there as the board opens, never an empty board); money/percent stickers only on money words; nothing moves after it lands (no zooms, push-ins, pulses); no music, no SFX — the voice is the only sound; camera zone stays empty; board copy that contradicts the presenter is flagged, never silently changed; every correction goes into `checks.md` as a DO or a DON'T.
- **QA loop:** read the QA report, fix, re-run only that board (`--only N`), until clean. Then watch the preview end to end.
- **Setup (once, free):** `bash skills/board-sync/setup.sh` installs Python packages + Playwright + Chromium (only if no Chrome exists) + checks ffmpeg. First transcription, if ever needed, downloads a ~1.6 GB speech model. No paid service, no sign-up.
- **How we use it (our work):** title/poster frames, subscribe banners and caption boards for films and Shorts can be built as code boards and synced to the voice with Whisper, then exported with Chrome + FFmpeg — free and repeatable. The 74 boards are a starting library (line/bar charts, KPI rows, rankings, versus cards, kinetic quotes, chapter dividers, price cards, node graphs). NOTE: boards are 1920×1080 landscape — for our 9:16 vertical reels, build vertical variants (1080×1920 canvas, same tokens) or use boards for 16:9 film segments and title cards.
- Status 2026-11-13 — export TESTED and WORKING. Setup: `bash skills/board-sync/setup.sh` with `BOARD_SYNC_NO_UV=1` installed the venv + packages (faster-whisper, playwright, numpy, pillow, static-ffmpeg) fine, but Playwright's own browser download kept timing out (its 30 s limit). ## 7. Prompting (Jeff Su)

Source: "Top 6 AI Productivity Tips for Work!", transcript in ~/workspace/research_notes/transcripts/9Hwanevuhd8.txt.

- Every prompt carries all six: **Task** (action verb, end goal), **Context** (who, background, constraints), **Exemplars** (examples of the wanted output), **Persona** (who the AI acts as), **Format** (structure, length), **Tone**.
- **The Markdown Shortcut (exact method):** AI wastes tokens processing PDF layout. Select all text in the PDF, copy, paste into a blank Google Doc, File → Download → Markdown. His 42-page tax guide went from 661 KB to 94 KB (90% smaller). Or: mount the folder in the desktop AI app and ask it to extract the relevant parts into a Markdown file. Rule: give AI the plain-text version of any mostly-text file (Markdown not Word/PDF, CSV not messy Excel). Keep the original as fallback; tell the AI to read Markdown by default and open the PDF only when it needs more. Store originals in a `source PDFs` folder; put the rule in the instruction file (AGENTS.md / CLAUDE.md).
- **The Folder Map (exact prompt):** "Read every file in this folder once, including the screenshots, and create INDEX.md. For each file, list its location, a one-sentence description, and when to use it." The AI checks the index first and opens only the files a task needs. Set a scheduled task to rescan so the index stays fresh.
- **Prompt Shortcuts (text expander vs Skills):** use a text expander for prompts you tweak before sending (he types `gptrephrase` for three clearer versions of an awkward email; `gptintent` for the intent check below); use **Skills for multi-step tasks that run the exact same way every time** (his Inbox Triage Skill is 2,533 words; he runs it by typing `/inbox-triage`). When a new model drops, ask the AI to update the Skill itself. Free expanders: Raycast (Mac), Beeftext (Windows).
- **The Intent Check (exact method):** before a high-stakes task, have the AI repeat your underlying intent back to you and propose a plan for your review. Prompt (with files attached): "Review all attached materials and the full context of my message, even if the information is rough, fragmented, or unfiltered. Repeat my underlying intent back to me and propose a plan of action for my review. Keep the plan proportionate to the goal." His example: three wedding-planning proposals — the first answer gave a recommendation with no scoring; after the intent check he could see the plan had no scorecard, so he replied "the plan is approved provided that you also create a scorecard with five criteria", and got a weighted scorecard. Correcting early is far cheaper than discovering a misunderstanding halfway.
- **The Spare Key:** give AI an API key and describe the change in plain English — the AI writes the code the app's API needs. Useful when an app has no plugin or connector. **Never paste an API key into a prompt** — store it in the OS keychain/credential manager and let the AI use it without seeing it.
- **Tool Rulebooks:** connecting Gmail/Slack/Calendar only gives access; a rulebook teaches the AI to use them the way YOU would. One folder per tool with a main instruction file (CLAUDE.md / AGENTS.md) plus per-tool rulebooks, e.g. "events default to 45 minutes". His Gmail example: with the rulebook the draft came out short, first sentence bolded, no sign-off (Gmail adds the signature). Ask the AI to draft each rulebook from how you already use the tool.
- **Lightning round:** (1) organise feedback with hashtags (`## Slide 2` above comments on slide 2, `###chart` for that slide's chart); (2) one good example beats an over-engineered prompt (paste last month's report, say "same format"); (3) approve a small sample before the whole batch (first 5 of 200 files, then the rest); (4) let one AI check another's work before you do (paste a ChatGPT draft into Claude and ask it to flag mistakes).
- **Prompt Reversal:** feed the AI your best output and ask it to reverse-engineer the perfect prompt.
- **Red Team:** get the AI to critique its own work like a ruthless editor; or let one AI check another's work before you judge it.
- **5-Minute Amplifier:** turn one piece of content into posts, snippets, scripts and more — one film becomes Shorts, Reels, captions, thumbnails.
- **Blueprint Scaffolding:** force the model to outline its logic before generating the final output.
- NotebookLM (now Gemini Notebook) for research: upload your own sources (notes, transcripts, gate reports), get citation-grounded answers, audio/video overviews, mind maps. Use it when hallucination tolerance is near-zero and information is scattered.

## 9. Titles (Nate Herk, measured over 2 years of uploads)

- Titles about **what happened/did** hit 38% vs 15% for other titles.
- Uploads **20+ minutes** hit 31% vs 13%.
- **Free-template** titles 40% vs 18%. **No-code** titles 36% vs 18%. Name the tool in the title.
- Apply where honest: lead with what happened, promise a real free resource, name the tool.

## 13. Five content-growth creators — Dua reel (2026-11-15)

Source: Dua Harshil (@dua.mov) reel https://www.instagram.com/reel/De_zKN0ORaS/ ("5 videos to become disgustingly good at content creation in 2026"). Full notes: ~/workspace/research_notes/dua-reel-five-creators-learnings.md. the user asked Muse to watch the five and learn deeply; the notes were built from full transcripts and deep breakdowns, not titles. These five are content-growth creators, not AI-tool creators — the rules below are about hooks, scripts, voice and pace.

- **Varun Mayya (Content Masterclass, 10-part Content Course):** distribution > product — distribution brings users → feedback → better product → talent/investors. Content is a long-term asset and a lightning rod; luck is engineered by increasing surface area. Trust through repetition (7-11-4: repeated interactions across platforms over time). Give value before selling. Think in years, not weeks. Build in public (share why, mistakes, decisions — people judge how you think).
- **AniThing / Aninda Chakraborty (~991K subs, mobile filmmaking):** story first, gear second — built the channel largely on a phone. Confidence on camera comes from knowing the story. Transfers to us as VOICE confidence: narration must sound like telling a friend a story, not reading a script. (Video verified 2026-11-15: "How to be Super CONFIDENT on YouTube. When You're NOT", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gij6OJgE2EA, 24 Apr 2022, 335,939 views.)
- **Kane Kallaway (Killer Hooks, 1B+ views, 367 videos):** the hook is the 80/20 — spend 80% of scripting time on the first 5 seconds. **3-beat formula:** Context Lean → Scroll-Stop Interjection (but/however/yet) → Contrarian Snapback. **4-mistake diagnostic (a hook must clear all four):** Delay (topic too late), Confusion (rewrite to 6th-grade level, active voice), Irrelevance (swap I/me → you/your; frame need-to-have), Disinterest (fix with A-vs-B contrast, stated when the audience baseline is uncertain). **6 archetypes:** Fortune Teller, Experimenter, Teacher, Magician (layer only, never standalone), Investigator, Contrarian — pick from the key visual, not the topic; any idea fits any archetype. **4-component alignment:** spoken + visual + text + audio must agree; viewers read visual → audio → visual; the key visual is load-bearing ("if you don't have a good visual, think: should I make this video?"). Visual hook = 3–5 word bold overlay + just enough motion. Hook sentences staccato, ≤12 words. AI is a clarity rewriter only ("rewrite this in a sixth grade reading level so there's no misunderstanding"), never a hook generator. Structure: hook → dance (setup, rehook, repeat) → hook again at the end so they share.
- **Mino Lee (200K followers in 1 year, 90 min/day; 0→250K YouTube subs in 1 year):** 27%-better hook at 3 s = 54x views (298K vs 5K). **One real ideal viewer:** a single real person (his friend Cle) — know their problems, beliefs and objections, answer the objections in the script. Energy: calm FaceTime call with a friend, never loud/performative. Pre-filming: deep breath, visualise that person in the room, shake out stiffness; hit record and don't stop — first 1–3 min will be stiff, accept it. **Minimum viable editing:** b-roll changes every sentence; **SLC captions — Short (2–3 words max), Large, Clear** (white, blurred black shadow); trending music, INSTRUMENTALS only (lyrics compete with the voice). **Community is the most important factor:** passive watching isn't learning; his 4 phases — Fries (1,000 views/video consistently), Pebbles (first 10K-view video), Gem (first 100K-view video → 10K followers), Money. First point of a video in under 55 seconds; put proof in the hook.
- **Tatsuki (consistency):** "sustainability is how you get to consistency." Burning out after ~8 weeks of weekly videos is the most common failure, not a moral failing. You LITERALLY CANNOT be consistent at a pace you can't maintain. Whatever pace you can hold right now IS the baseline (even 1 video/month + 3 posts/week); raise frequency only once the current pace is boringly sustainable. Applied to us: our intense pace only works because the SYSTEM carries it (parallel agents, day-ahead builds, scheduled jobs) — never by willpower or more hours.
- **How to use this section:** the hook agent in the ten-agent gate runs Kallaway's 4-mistake diagnostic + 3-beat formula + archetype-from-visual from the next productions onward (Ep. 4, Letters Part 9+, Green Valley Part 4 — not a redo of already-gated uploads). **Poster hook rule (approved by the user 2026-11-15):** the poster frame carries the contrast — most shocking frame + 3–5 word bold overlay, voice speaks the lean line with the title inside it. Captions stay SLC (2–3 words/chunk). Narration direction: one real listener, FaceTime energy, calm. Viral Daily: rotate hook archetypes, keep the frame-1 proof chip.

Use `short-form-edit` for reels/Shorts from a talking-head recording, `edit-video` for a full edit, `cut-silences` / `cut-mistakes` for single operations, `motion-showreel` for a brand reel cut to music. Export one board: `python tools/export.py boards/D44Line.html --seconds 8 -o out/line.mp4`. Full pipeline: Whisper timestamps → board-brief → board-system → board-sync → export. See §5 for the rules.

Use it to break down a competitor's video shot by shot before making our version (the rea-first rule for Viral Daily). Note: a full 17-minute video is too heavy for this machine (OOM) — decompose 2–3 minute trims (hook + one method demo) instead, with `--workers 1 --no-stems`.
