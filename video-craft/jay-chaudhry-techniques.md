## The core idea

Direct the AI like a film crew, don't prompt it like a search box. "I didn't ask AI to make this film.
I directed it…" — "It's all just a prompt" is the biggest misconception: "Where does the camera go?
What lens are we on? When do we cut? … And when AI gets one of those things wrong, you direct it again."

## The 10 lessons (condensed)

1. **Emotion is the product; the picture is only the delivery.** Ask first: "How do I want someone to feel…" — then build the story to elicit that.
2. **Your own history is your biggest audience.** His Pakistan-history posts broke out ~100× over his fiction, same account, same format: Captain (Imran Khan) 30,000 likes; ALAM trailer 7,700 likes / 390 comments; typical AUGMENTED fiction reels 50–210 likes.
3. **One idea, one moment, one question per piece.** Every episode is one "what if"; every cut-down clip is one relatable moment. If the poster hook needs two sentences, the film has two ideas.
4. **Cut the long story into one-moment clips, on a fixed slot.** A 90-minute documentary becomes short segments, one moment each; AUGMENTED releases weekly; a vlog every Tuesday, a cooking show every Thursday.
5. **Pre-visualise everything, then direct.** AI storyboard first, lock visuals before building, roadmap per scene — then film. Generative fill in post only finalises the look.
6. **Blend real and fiction — never lose realism.** Make a moment "real enough to make you question it for a second". Restraint over spectacle.
7. **Make the place a character; show pain with dignity, pointed at the triumph.** Toronto shapes his Street Scales story the way the Green Valley shapes ours. Never exploit pain — focus on the comeback.
8. **Fans make you relevant, not your record.** "You want subscriptions?? Give the audience what they want." The relationship is the metric.
9. **Teach the craft in public.** Manifesto, director's notes, prompt sheets shown on screen in the process films. His producer explainer is his 3rd-most-commented post — teaching builds a second audience.
10. **AI production is cheaper, but it's not cheap.** Pre- and post-production still cost (writing, creative development, editing, sound design) plus premium tool pricing. Invent constraints to force originality — he invented a whole prehistoric language so the AI couldn't lean on English, and directed purely through expression, rhythm and body language.

## How it applies here

- **Gate line 0 for every film and Short:** "How do I want someone to feel?" — answered in one sentence before any frames are made.
- **One "what if" per film**, checked through the poster hook (the poster-hook rule already does this).
- **Storyboard lock before building** — the same discipline as the FilmCraft Part 7 lock step. Never skip the lock under time pressure.
- **Fixed slots** for recurring formats (GTA clips 02:00 / 05:00 / 17:00 / 20:00 AST; Geolyth roundup ~07:00) — the audience learns when to come back.
- **One "how this film was made" Short per film** (show the lock sheets / prompt sheets on screen, not just the result).
- **Fictional characters are a structural advantage:** his Captain fight (likeness rejected, name triggering filters) is a problem invented characters never have. Keep ours invented.
- **Pin the shop window:** best film, manifesto-equivalent post, craft / process post — his pinned trio, copied.
